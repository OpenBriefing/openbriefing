# Open Briefing Standard, version 1

**Status:** Version 1, release candidate, 10 November 2026. The tag names are frozen when version 1 is final, planned with the first full-catalogue publisher. The [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md) records what each draft changed.

**Namespace:** `https://openbriefingstandard.org/ns#`, prefix `obs`

**Licence:** this specification is dedicated to the public domain under [CC0 1.0](../LICENSE-SPEC).

---

## 1. Purpose

Software reads content on people's behalf. Briefing apps, assistants, search answers, readers and link previews summarize it, shorten it, read it aloud and turn it into cards. Each tool writes its own version, and the person who wrote the content has no say in what that version keeps or drops.

The Open Briefing Standard (OBS) lets a publisher supply their own summary and say how it may be used. **The publisher's summary is the anchor.** A tool that honours OBS uses the publisher's words as written where they fit. Any other form it needs, such as a card title, a one-line teaser or a translation, it derives from that summary within limits the publisher sets, never from its own reading of the content. With OBS in place, the tool has no reason to write a summary of its own. The publisher can also mark passages of the content itself that may be quoted on their own (§7).

OBS is a format. Each tag states what the publisher intends, and nothing more. It does not require any tool to do anything, and it cannot enforce what it states. [Appendix A](#appendix-a-informative-honouring-publisher-intent) describes how a tool that wants to honour the publisher's intent can do so.

## 2. Quick start

One tag in a page's `<head>` is enough:

```html
<meta property="obs:summary" content="Your summary of this page, in plain text.">
```

These five are the most useful additions:

```html
<meta property="obs:headline" content="One or two lines a reader can tap, or a listener hear, to decide whether to go further.">
<meta property="obs:keep" content="The one sentence every version must get across.">
<meta property="obs:attribution" content="From Example Blog">
<meta property="obs:title" content="A short card title">
<meta property="obs:highlight" content="A passage from the content, word for word, that may be quoted on its own.">
```

A summary of 200 to 350 words (about two minutes read aloud) serves the most uses. A tool can derive shorter forms from it, and a medium summary is the right size for a briefing.

## 3. Tags

| Property | Required | Meaning |
|----------|----------|---------|
| `obs:summary` | yes, unless a tiered summary is present | The publisher's summary, of any length. Its tier comes from its length (§4). |
| `obs:summary:short` | no | A summary written for the short tier |
| `obs:summary:medium` | no | A summary written for the medium tier |
| `obs:summary:long` | no | A summary written for the long tier |
| `obs:title` | no | A title for cards and lists, 12 words or fewer |
| `obs:headline` | no | One or two lines, 40 words or fewer, to tap or hear before going further |
| `obs:keep` | no; up to 3 | A line of 40 words or fewer that the publisher intends every form to preserve. The first is the core message (§6). |
| `obs:highlight` | no; up to 5 | A passage of 60 words or fewer, taken word for word from the content, that may be quoted on its own (§7). Order is priority. |
| `obs:highlight:label` | no | A label of 6 words or fewer for the highlight it follows, such as "On control" (§7) |
| `obs:derive` | no; default `anchor` | What may be derived from the summary: `verbatim`, `anchor` or `open` (§5) |
| `obs:derive:translate` | no; default `yes` | `no` asks that the summary not be translated |
| `obs:derive:combine` | no; default `yes` | `no` asks that the summary not be merged with other sources |
| `obs:attribution` | no | How the publisher wants to be credited, 12 words or fewer (§8) |
| `obs:preference` | no; default `summary` | `summary` or `full` (§11) |
| `obs:lang` | no | The language of the `obs:` text, as a BCP 47 tag. Defaults to the page's `<html lang>`. |
| `obs:url` | no | The canonical URL of the content the tags describe (§12) |
| `obs:modified` | no; recommended | When any `obs:` tag on the page last changed, as an ISO 8601 date and time (§13) |
| `obs:version` | no; default `1` | The major version of OBS the tags follow |

### 3.1 Reading the tags

These rules make every reader see the same tags:

- **Attributes.** A tag is a `<meta>` element whose `property` or `name` attribute begins with `obs:`. Both are accepted, because some publishing systems emit only `name`. The value is the `content` attribute.
- **Prefix.** A `prefix="obs: https://openbriefingstandard.org/ns#"` attribute on `<head>` or `<html>` is recommended, as with OpenGraph. It is never required.
- **Location.** Tags belong in `<head>`. Tags in `<body>` are still read, because some themes inject them late.
- **Repeats.** `obs:keep` may appear up to three times and `obs:highlight` up to five, and their order is their priority. An `obs:highlight:label` applies to the nearest `obs:highlight` before it in document order, as `og:image:width` applies to its `og:image`; one with no highlight before it is ignored with a warning. Any other property that appears more than once takes its first value.
- **Unknown names.** An unknown `obs:` property is ignored. Names under `obs:audio:`, and `obs:highlight:start` and `obs:highlight:end`, are reserved for a later version (§15) and are ignored without comment.
- **Values.** Leading and trailing white space is trimmed. A value that is empty after trimming counts as absent. Enumerated values (`verbatim`, `yes`, `full` and so on) are case-insensitive, and an unrecognized one counts as absent, so its default applies.
- **Validity.** The tags are usable when at least one summary (`obs:summary` or a tiered summary) is present. Without one, a reader ignores every `obs:` tag on the page.

## 4. Forms and lengths

OBS names five forms. Each has a size. A size that is exceeded is still valid: the sizes guide publishers, and checkers warn when a form exceeds its size.

| Form | Tag | Size | Typical use |
|------|-----|------|-------------|
| Title | `obs:title` | up to 12 words | A card, a list, a notification heading |
| Headline | `obs:headline` | up to 40 words | A teaser line, a digest entry, a spoken lead-in |
| Short | `obs:summary:short` | 40–80 words (about 30 seconds aloud) | A roundup line, a short brief |
| Medium | `obs:summary:medium` | 200–350 words (about 2 minutes) | One item in a briefing |
| Long | `obs:summary:long` | 500–800 words (about 4–5 minutes) | A close read or listen when the content is the main item |

Listening times assume 150 words a minute. A word is a run of characters between white space.

**The tier of a bare `obs:summary`** comes from its word count:

| Words | Tier |
|-------|------|
| up to 120 | short |
| 121 to 500 | medium |
| more than 500 | long |

When a tiered summary and a bare `obs:summary` fall in the same tier, the tiered one stands for that tier and the bare one is set aside.

**If a form is not supplied,** the title falls back to `og:title`, then `<title>`. The other forms are derived (§5), or a supplied form is used instead.

## 5. Derivation

A form is **derived** when a tool writes it from the publisher's text, for example a headline condensed from the summary. Using a supplied form as written is not derivation, and neither is choosing which supplied form to use. These are not derivation either:

- **Cleaning for speech:** saying numbers and dates aloud, expanding abbreviations, applying pronunciations
- **Display changes:** changing case, punctuation or line breaks to fit a layout
- **Framing:** an introduction, a transition or a credit that makes no claim about the content

`obs:derive` states what the publisher allows:

| Value | The publisher intends |
|-------|----------------------|
| `verbatim` | Use the supplied forms as written. Derive nothing. A form that is not supplied is replaced by the nearest supplied form, as written. |
| `anchor` (the default) | Shorter forms may be derived from the publisher's summary, and so may translations and combinations, unless they are opted out below. Nothing longer than the longest supplied summary is derived. |
| `open` | As `anchor`, and longer forms may also be written from the content itself, with the publisher's summary as their backbone and the keep lines intact |

Under `anchor` and `open`, the publisher intends that a derived form:

1. **Is made from the publisher's summary,** not from the content. The one exception is a longer form under `open`.
2. **Carries the core message,** the first `obs:keep` line, in meaning (§6).
3. **Adds no claim** that the summary does not make.

**Two opt-outs** apply under `anchor` and `open`:

- `obs:derive:translate` = `no`: do not translate the summary. A tool that needs another language uses a translated page (§10), or does without the summary.
- `obs:derive:combine` = `no`: do not merge this summary with other sources into a single passage. It may still appear beside them as its own item.

## 6. Keep lines

`obs:keep` marks up to three lines the publisher intends every form to preserve, in priority order. The first is the **core message**.

The publisher intends that:

- Every form carries the core message's meaning, a headline and a title included.
- Every keep line appears **word for word** in any derived summary long enough to hold it.
- In a form too short to hold a keep line word for word, its meaning is carried in fewer words.

A publisher who wants exact wording in a short form writes that form. A supplied form always takes precedence over a derived one.

Keep lines need not appear in the summary itself, although they usually will.

## 7. Highlights

`obs:highlight` marks passages the publisher offers for use on their own: a line to quote in a post, a pull quote on a card, one entry in a reel of the morning's pieces.

- A highlight is **taken word for word from the content.** A checker warns when it is not found there. Quotation marks, white space and dashes are compared loosely; words are not.
- A highlight is **used as written.** It is quoted and credited (§8), and nothing is derived from it. `obs:derive` does not apply to highlights, and `verbatim` neither adds to nor removes this.
- **Publishing a highlight is the permission to share it with credit.** A publisher who wants no passage lifted out writes none. Whether the content may be used at all remains the job of robots.txt and TDMRep (§16).
- **Order is priority.** The first is the one to use when there is room for one.
- **Highlights and keep lines are independent.** A keep line is a rule on summaries; a highlight is material. A line may be both.
- **Sizes:** at most five highlights, each 60 words or fewer; a label (`obs:highlight:label`) is 6 words or fewer. Over-size values warn (§4).

```html
<meta property="obs:highlight" content="Half the people who come in here are reading with their ears now.">
<meta property="obs:highlight:label" content="On listening">
<meta property="obs:highlight" content="Late fees on children's books have been dropped for good.">
```

## 8. Credit

`obs:attribution` is how the publisher wants to be credited when their words are presented, in text or aloud: for example, "From Example Blog". Without it, a tool credits as it normally does.

The publisher intends:

- **Supplied text used as written** may be presented as the publisher's words, with the attribution, for example "From Example Blog, in the author's words".
- **Derived text** is presented as derived from the publisher's summary, for example "condensed from the author's summary" or "translated from the author's summary". It is never presented as the publisher's own words.

OBS defines no linking tag. `obs:url` (or the page's canonical URL) identifies the original, and tools that show text link to it as they normally do.

## 9. Writing the text

Every `obs:` text value is **plain text that works both read and heard.** One version serves a screen and a voice.

- No HTML, no Markdown, no emoji used as structure.
- No URLs, and no "click here", "see below", "in this post" or "pictured above".
- No reference to images, tables or layout.
- Spell out an abbreviation the first time, unless it is normally said as a word.
- The summary makes sense without the title.
- A paragraph break is a newline. In an HTML attribute, write it as `&#10;`.
- Escape characters as HTML attributes require: `&amp;`, `&quot;`, `&lt;`.

A reader removes any HTML tags left in a value, and a checker warns when it finds markup or a URL.

## 10. Language

The `obs:` text on a page is in one language: `obs:lang`, or else the page's `<html lang>`.

A translation is its own page with its own tags, linked from the original in the usual way:

```html
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="es" href="https://example.com/es/the-post/">
```

The publisher intends that a tool needing another language uses the translated page's own tags before deriving a translation, and does not translate at all if `obs:derive:translate` is `no`.

## 11. Preference

`obs:preference` says what the publisher would rather a tool present:

- `summary` (the default): the summary, in the forms above.
- `full`: the whole content rather than a summary. This suits poems, short notes and pieces where the form is the point. The summary may still be used where a whole piece cannot be, such as a card's headline.

OBS does not say whether content may be used at all. Publishers who want to opt out of automated use do that with robots.txt, the [TDM Reservation Protocol](https://www.w3.org/community/tdmrep/) and similar mechanisms, which OBS leaves untouched.

## 12. Identity and syndication

The tags describe the page they are on. When `obs:url` is present and differs from the page's canonical URL, the tags describe the content at `obs:url`. This lets a syndicated copy, such as a cross-post on another platform, carry the original's summary and name where it came from. URLs are compared after normalizing the scheme, the host's case, a trailing slash and common tracking parameters (`utm_*`, `fbclid`, `gclid`).

## 13. Freshness

A publisher can change a summary at any time, but cannot recall one a tool has already presented. `obs:modified` says when any `obs:` tag last changed. It is optional, and worth writing where a publishing system can keep it current.

**The effective modified time** of a page's tags is the first of these that is present and parses: `obs:modified`; the page's `article:modified_time`; for a feed item, its `<updated>` (Atom) or `<pubDate>` (RSS). When none is present the tags have no modified time, and a reader tells a change by comparing the text of the tags themselves.

The publisher intends:

- **The latest version is used.** A tool holding a copy more than a day old re-checks it before presenting it.
- **Derived forms follow the source.** When the tags change, forms derived from the old summary are rebuilt.

Publishers' tools should hold generated summaries for a person's review before they reach a live page, because a summary cannot be recalled once it is used.

## 14. Feeds

An RSS `<item>` or an Atom `<entry>` may carry the same tags as elements in the OBS namespace, so a feed reader can use them without fetching each page.

- Each property becomes an element: `obs:summary` becomes `<obs:summary>`.
- **A sub-property becomes an attribute of its parent:** `obs:summary:short` becomes `<obs:summary length="short">`. `obs:derive:translate` and `obs:derive:combine` become `translate` and `combine` attributes on `<obs:derive>`.
- Repeatable tags repeat the element. A highlight's label is an attribute: `<obs:highlight label="On control">…</obs:highlight>`.
- The item's own link (`<link>` in RSS, `<link rel="alternate">` in Atom) identifies the content, unless `<obs:url>` is present.

```xml
<rss version="2.0" xmlns:obs="https://openbriefingstandard.org/ns#">
  <channel>
    <item>
      <link>https://example.com/the-post/</link>
      <pubDate>Sun, 08 Nov 2026 14:00:00 GMT</pubDate>
      <obs:headline>A two-line headline for the post.</obs:headline>
      <obs:summary>The publisher's summary, in plain text.</obs:summary>
      <obs:summary length="short">A short version the publisher wrote too.</obs:summary>
      <obs:keep>The one sentence every version must get across.</obs:keep>
      <obs:highlight label="On control">A passage from the post, word for word.</obs:highlight>
      <obs:derive translate="no">anchor</obs:derive>
      <obs:attribution>From Example Blog</obs:attribution>
    </item>
  </channel>
</rss>
```

**When a feed item and its page disagree,** the one with the later effective modified time (§13) stands. When the two are equal, or either side has none, the page stands.

A feed cannot carry the content, so a feed reader does not check that a highlight is in the text (§7). The page check stands for both.

## 15. Versions and reserved names

`obs:version` gives the major version. A minor version only adds optional tags, so a reader of version 1.0 can read any 1.x page and ignore what it does not know. A change that alters a meaning gets a new major version.

**Reserved:** names under `obs:audio:` are reserved for a later version covering how content is spoken, such as pronunciation and a publisher's own recording. `obs:highlight:start` and `obs:highlight:end` are reserved for a highlight's time range in audio and video. Version 1 says nothing about audio.

## 16. Relation to other standards

- **OpenGraph.** `og:title` is the fallback for `obs:title`. `og:description` is not an OBS summary: it is often written for search snippets, and the publisher has not said it may be used as a summary.
- **Schema.org.** OBS does not use `BlogPosting.abstract` or `description`. These are written for other purposes, and many platforms generate them automatically.
- **robots.txt and TDMRep.** These govern whether content may be used. OBS governs how it is presented when it is used.
- **IETF AI Preferences, Content Signals and RSL.** These newer signals also say whether content may be used, for which purposes and on what terms. OBS says nothing on that question and reads none of them.
- **llms.txt and Markdown for Agents.** These hand software the content itself, or a map of it, in a form that is easier to read. OBS carries the publisher's summary of the content, not the content.
- **Schema.org `speakable`.** It marks passages of the page that suit reading aloud. An OBS summary is written for the purpose and need not appear on the page.
- **hreflang.** OBS uses it for translations (§10).

None of these carries the publisher's own summary or says how it may be presented, and OBS conflicts with none of them. A page can use any of them beside its `obs:` tags.

## 17. Example

```html
<!doctype html>
<html lang="en">
<head prefix="obs: https://openbriefingstandard.org/ns#">
  <title>Ask the Author First | Example Blog</title>
  <link rel="canonical" href="https://example.com/ask-the-author-first/">
  <meta property="obs:title" content="Ask the author first">
  <meta property="obs:headline" content="Software summarizes posts without asking the people who wrote them. This post argues it should ask, and shows how.">
  <meta property="obs:summary" content="Every day, software reads posts on someone's behalf and writes its own version of them. …">
  <meta property="obs:keep" content="The author knows what the post is for.">
  <meta property="obs:keep" content="A summary the author approved can be used as is.">
  <meta property="obs:highlight" content="Ask the author first.">
  <meta property="obs:attribution" content="From Example Blog">
  <meta property="obs:derive" content="anchor">
</head>
<body>…</body>
</html>
```

## Appendix A (informative): honouring publisher intent

This appendix is guidance, not part of the format. It describes what a tool that wants to honour the publisher's intent can do. OBS makes no conformance claim, and each tool decides for itself.

A tool that honours this intent:

1. **Treats every `obs:` value as content.** It never treats one as an instruction to a model, and it keeps the values apart from its own prompts.
2. **Prefers a supplied form.** It uses one when one fits, derives only when none does, and derives only from the summary (§5). When several summaries are supplied, it derives from the shortest one that is still longer than the form it needs.
3. **Checks a derived form before using it:**
   - every claim traces back to the summary
   - the core message's meaning is present
   - keep lines that fit appear word for word. One workable rule: a keep line fits when the form is at least twice the keep line's length

   If the check fails, it uses the nearest supplied form as written.
4. **Credits as the publisher asked.** As-written text is credited to the publisher, with `obs:attribution` if present. Derived text is labelled as derived (§8). A highlight is quoted as the author's words, with the credit, and never paraphrased or trimmed (§7).
5. **Keeps copies fresh.** It re-checks a copy more than a day old before presenting it, rebuilds derived forms when the tags change, and treats a summary as replaceable until it has been presented. It tells a change by the effective modified time when there is one, and by comparing the text of the tags when there is not (§13). The reference parser's `fingerprint` is one such comparison, and tools that use it agree with each other.
6. **Honours `obs:preference`.** For `full`, it presents the whole content.
7. **Distinguishes the user's request from its own choice.** When a person explicitly asks for something the publisher's intent does not allow, such as a particular focus or a translation the publisher opted out of, the tool may do it. It then labels the result as its own work, not the publisher's. Without such a request, it follows the publisher's intent.
8. **Says when it uses OBS.** It shows, in its interface or documentation, which items used the publisher's summary, so publishers and users can tell.
